# Medical Translation

## Scope

Translate or closely paraphrase the requested components:

- title, citation, and structured abstract;
- background, current evidence, research gap, objective, and hypothesis;
- design, setting, population, eligibility, intervention/exposure/test, comparator, and outcomes;
- specimen collection, assay or imaging platform, reagents, storage, quality control, and analytical performance;
- statistical or computational methods;
- results, subgroup and sensitivity analyses, harms, and null findings;
- discussion, strengths, limitations, applicability, and conclusion.

Adapt headings to the article type. A guideline, meta-analysis, qualitative study, or basic-science paper does not need to mimic a clinical trial structure.

## Terminology Workflow

1. Extract recurring terms, abbreviations, drug names, gene/protein names, instruments, and outcome definitions.
2. Build a compact bilingual glossary before long-form translation.
3. Use authoritative Chinese terminology for the specialty when available.
4. Preserve internationally standardized names, gene symbols, units, and statistical notation.
5. Expand an abbreviation at first use, then use it consistently.

## Accuracy Rules

- Preserve design terms such as randomized, prospective, retrospective, cross-sectional, diagnostic, prognostic, exploratory, post hoc, and hypothesis-generating.
- Preserve every number, unit, group, n/N, percentage, mean or median, SD/IQR, effect estimate, confidence interval, P value, time point, dose, platform, and analysis population.
- Keep intention-to-treat, per-protocol, missing-data, multiplicity, and adjustment language intact.
- Split long English sentences when useful, but preserve negation, conditions, exceptions, and logical qualifiers.
- Preserve negative and null findings; do not translate only significant results.
- Distinguish odds, risk, rate, hazard, prevalence, incidence, accuracy, discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility.
- Do not replace author uncertainty with stronger Chinese wording.

## Discrepancies

When abstract, prose, table, figure, supplement, registry, or protocol disagree:

1. record each conflicting value and location;
2. quote only the shortest necessary English fragment;
3. choose a value only when a justified source hierarchy exists;
4. state the choice and reason in a translator note;
5. never silently harmonize conflicting data.

Prefer prespecified primary analyses and primary result tables over an obvious prose typo, but label that judgment.

## Output Structure

Use the applicable sections:

```text
Citation
Translation note and glossary
Title
Structured abstract
Research background and gap
Research objective or question
Methods
Results
Discussion
Strengths and limitations
Author conclusion
Discrepancies and translator notes
```

Keep practice implications outside the translation unless requested. Label inferred content separately from the author's text.

## Copyright And Privacy

Work from user-provided or lawfully accessible sources. Avoid reproducing long English passages when a faithful Chinese rendering or structured close reading meets the need. Do not include patient-identifiable or confidential information in translations or presentation materials.
