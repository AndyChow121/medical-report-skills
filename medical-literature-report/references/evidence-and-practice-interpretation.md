# Evidence Boundaries And Practice Interpretation

## Evidence Classes

Classify every claim before using it:

| Class | Meaning | Allowed language |
|---|---|---|
| Direct result | measured or analyzed in this study | found, observed, was associated |
| Author interpretation | interpretation in the discussion | authors propose, may reflect |
| External evidence | separately cited or verified evidence | previous studies reported |
| Presenter inference | derived for professional practice | based on this paper, consider |

Never mix these classes in one unlabeled statement.

## Interpretation Boundaries

- Observational evidence requires association language.
- Prediction evidence must separate association, discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility.
- Exploratory cutoffs are not validated diagnostic or treatment thresholds.
- Cell, animal, omics, and AI findings require independent biological and clinical validation.
- Subgroup claims require attention to interaction tests and multiplicity.
- A nonsignificant result does not prove equivalence or no effect.
- Statistical significance does not establish patient importance, feasibility, or cost-effectiveness.
- Numbers in result figures (HR/CI/AUC/I²) must be cross-checked against the article body and supplements via `graph-interpretation` (see `figures-interpretation.md`). Any mismatch belongs in the discrepancy log.

## Professional Adaptation

Select only implications relevant to the audience.

### Clinical

Consider target population, absolute benefit or harm, comparator, feasibility, patient preferences, guideline consistency, and implementation prerequisites. Do not turn one paper into a treatment recommendation.

### Laboratory And Diagnostic Services

Consider specimen timing, preanalytics, platform and reagent, calibration and traceability, analytical performance, reference intervals or decision limits, cross-platform transferability, quality control, incremental value, and reporting workflow. Say `not reported` rather than treating missing method details as measured failures.

### Nursing And Allied Health

Consider workflow, monitoring, education, adherence, safety, patient-reported outcomes, staffing, and setting-specific feasibility without expanding beyond studied interventions.

### Pharmacy

Consider dose, formulation, interaction, adherence, adverse events, subgroup response, comparative effectiveness, pharmacoeconomics, and medication-use process.

### Public Health

Consider population representativeness, absolute burden, equity, implementation scale, resource needs, surveillance, and policy context.

## Inference Sequence

Reason in this order:

1. What was directly measured or compared?
2. How internally valid and precise is the result?
3. Is it associative, predictive, causal, mechanistic, or exploratory?
4. Does the target setting match the study setting?
5. What validation or implementation evidence is still missing?
6. What could reasonably change now, and what should remain a research proposal?

Place this note on inference slides:

```text
以下启示为基于原文结果的延伸解读，并非作者直接结论或患者个体化医疗建议。
```

## Critical Appraisal Questions

- Is the target population the same as the intended audience's population?
- Could eligibility or recruitment create selection bias?
- Were intervention, exposure, test, and outcome measured in the correct order?
- Were methods and time points comparable across groups?
- Were missing data, confounding, multiplicity, and sensitivity analyses handled appropriately?
- Are effect estimates accompanied by uncertainty and absolute effects?
- Was validation independent and clinically relevant?
- Can the finding change practice now, or does it primarily motivate further validation?
